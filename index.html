Introduction to ArcLance
ArcLance is a Web3 payment platform built specifically for freelancers in countries where traditional payment services like PayPal, Payoneer, and Stripe are restricted or unavailable — including Pakistan, India, Bangladesh and Nigeria. It works in any country where you can hold a wallet.
Fast settlement
Payments confirm in seconds on Arc. No bank is involved.
Flat network fee
Flat network fee, no percentage cut.
Self-Custodial
Only you control your funds. ArcLance never touches your money.
What Problem Does ArcLance Solve?
Millions of talented freelancers in South Asia and Africa cannot receive payments from international clients because:

✗ PayPal is unavailable or heavily restricted in Pakistan, Bangladesh, and many African countries
✗ Payoneer charges 2–3% fees and requires lengthy KYC verification
✗ Wire transfers (SWIFT) take 3–5 days and cost $25–$45 per transaction
✗ Freelance marketplaces charge platform fees and may hold funds before releasing them

ArcLance solves this by using USDC on Arc Network — a blockchain specifically designed for stablecoin payments, with a flat fee and fast finality.
Currently on Testnet: ArcLance runs on Arc Testnet. All USDC used is test USDC with no real monetary value. This allows you to explore every feature safely before the mainnet launch.

Payment Flow — What Happens When You Send USDC
①You enter recipient address and amount in the Send form
②ArcLance calls the USDC smart contract's transfer(to, amount) function
③Your wallet (MetaMask/Rabby) signs the transaction — you approve it
④The signed transaction is broadcast to Arc Testnet nodes
⑤Arc confirms the transaction in seconds and USDC arrives in the recipient's wallet
✓Transaction hash recorded on-chain, viewable at testnet.arcscan.app
Why Arc Network?
Arc Network is a stablecoin-native EVM-compatible Layer 1 blockchain built and maintained by the Arc team. Unlike Ethereum (where gas is paid in ETH) or other L2s (where you need the native token), Arc pays all gas fees in USDC — making it ideal for freelancers who only hold stablecoins and don't want to buy volatile crypto tokens just to pay transaction fees.

Pay Link
Your personal payment page — share one link with any client, anywhere in the world.
What is a Pay Link?
A Pay Link is a shareable URL that contains your wallet address (or username), requested amount, and service label. Format: https://arclance.xyz/pay/<username-or-address>/<amount>/<label>. A username only works on the device where it was saved, so use your wallet address when you share a link with someone else. When a client opens it, they see a payment page where they can connect their wallet and pay you — without needing to know your 0x address.

Example: https://arclance.xyz/pay/ali-hassan/500/Logo-Design

Bridge Assets
Move USDC between Arc Testnet and other blockchains using Circle's CCTP protocol.
What is CCTP?
Circle Cross-Chain Transfer Protocol (CCTP) is Circle's official bridge for moving USDC between blockchains. It works by burning USDC on the source chain and minting fresh USDC on the destination — no wrapped tokens, no custodial risk. This is the most secure way to bridge USDC.
Example routes
Arc Testnet ↔ Ethereum Sepolia~15 min
Arc Testnet ↔ Base Sepolia~15 min
The Bridge page lists every network that is currently supported, for USDC and for EURC.

Username Registry
Register a human-readable @username on Arc Testnet so clients can pay you by name, not by 0x address.
Why Register a Username?
Instead of sharing 0x742d35Cc6634C0532925a3b8D70d4Ef..., you can share @ali-hassan. Easier to share, harder to mistype, and works for Pay Links too.
Registration
Go to Payments → Username tab, enter a 3–20 character name (lowercase a-z, digits 0-9, internal hyphens), and confirm the register(name) transaction in your wallet. Names are first-come-first-served (one per wallet, releasable anytime with release()) and testnet-only for now on Arc Testnet. No protocol fee is charged (only Arc Testnet gas in USDC).
Using a Username
In the Send form (@Username mode), Pay Links (/pay/<name>/...), and invoice links, ArcLance queries UsernameRegistry.resolve(name) on the Arc Testnet RPC and always shows the resolved short address (0x1234...5678) next to the name so the payer can verify it. Unregistered names show Name not registered and block sending.

FAQ
Frequently asked questions about ArcLance.
Do I need ETH to pay gas fees?
No. Arc Network's gas is paid in USDC — you never need to buy ETH or any other volatile token.
Is this real money?
ArcLance currently runs on Arc Testnet. All USDC is test USDC with no real value. Mainnet is planned for a future release.
What is USDC?
USDC is a regulated stablecoin issued by Circle (US-regulated). 1 USDC = $1.00 USD always. Backed by cash and US Treasuries; reserves are attested by independent accountants.
My client doesn't know crypto. Can they pay me?
Yes — they'll need MetaMask and USDC (one-time setup, ~15 minutes). Share the AI Assistant with them for guidance. Future versions will include a dedicated client onboarding flow.
Swap vs Bridge — what's the difference?
Swap = exchange token A for token B on the same chain (USDC → EURC on Arc). Bridge = move token across chains (USDC from Arc → Ethereum).
Can ArcLance freeze my funds?
ArcLance never holds your funds and cannot freeze them. USDC itself is issued by Circle, which can blacklist addresses under its own policies.
Is my wallet address public?
Yes — all blockchain transactions are publicly viewable on the block explorer (testnet.arcscan.app). This is normal for blockchains. Your name is not attached to your address unless you voluntarily share both.
How does the on-chain @username registry work?
Usernames are recorded on-chain in the UsernameRegistry contract on Arc Testnet (testnet-only for now). Registration is first-come-first-served with one name per wallet (3–20 lowercase letters, digits, or internal hyphens; reserved protocol names cannot be claimed), and the owner can call release() at any time. The Send form, Pay Links, and invoice links resolve names on-chain via resolve(name) and display the resolved short address next to the name so payers can verify it before sending.
